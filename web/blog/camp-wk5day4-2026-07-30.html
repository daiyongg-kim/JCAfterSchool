A Sentence Has a Skeleton 🦴
문장의 뼈대를 배운 날

JC After School · Coquitlam, BC · July 30, 2026 (Thu)
Analogies & the Body 🧠

Today the middle school students learned about the concept of analogies — and about how the body, or the skeleton, is an analogy for a sentence. A sentence is a complete thought or idea, written or spoken; a skeleton is what holds a body together and gives it its shape. Once you see it that way, the two start to look remarkably alike. 🦴

🔗 What Is an Analogy? This is to that, as this is to that

An analogy is a bridge between two pairs of things: tree is to leaf as human is to cell, heart is to beat as drum is to rhythm, shoes are to feet as gloves are to hands. The students worked through the examples one by one, and the margins of the worksheet filled up with their own additions — rice : water, apples : bananas, verbs : sentences, nouns : sentences — along with the words that go with the idea: ratio, proportional, hypothesis. 🧩

The whole day was mapped out on the board: finish the idiom books, handwriting, science — the skeletal system, analogy, sentences, writing. And off to one side, the long words broken into pieces to be read out loud: a-n-a-l-o-g-y, s-e-n-t-e-n-c-e, s-k-e-l-e-t-o-n. 📋

🔬 Science experiment
Can a person eat while standing on their hands or head — completely upside down?
Yes — of course!

Because our throat (esophageal) muscles work against gravity to move food down to the stomach. Food does not simply “fall” — it is squeezed along, whichever way up you happen to be.

Ian, Minwoo and Sua volunteered to put it to the test. 🙌

🦴 A Body, Traced Life-Size The primary class

Meanwhile the primary students worked on learning to write the words related to a body or skeleton — the start of their worksheet packs on building sentences, from the most basic all the way up to the more detailed. And to get there, they needed a body to look at. Leah was our model for the outline. ✏️

Then the words themselves. Skull, ribs, pelvis, arm, hand, foot — read them, write them, and draw an arrow to exactly the right place on the skeleton. Every word learned here becomes a word they can use in a sentence later. 🔤

🏅 Minwoo’s Record The most idioms in the class

And there was one more thing to finish today. Minwoo completed his compilation of idioms in his little flip-book — and in doing it, broke the class record for the most idioms in a book. 🎉

Flip it open and every page turns out the same way: the idiom on top, the meaning underneath, written out in his own words. “Let the cat out of the bag — accidentally told the secret.” “In hot water — in trouble, going to be punished.” “The apple of my eye — my most adored person.” Page after page after page. 📖

✒️

And something new: today we also introduced cursive to the students who were interested. Joined-up handwriting, one letter flowing into the next — an optional extra on the board, and there were takers straight away.

🍱 Lunch Katsu, gim and kimchi

Lunch today was crispy pork cutlet, cut into bite-size pieces, with sheets of gim (roasted seaweed) and kimchi to go with it. Rolled up in seaweed, dipped, stacked — everyone had their own method. 😋

🎨 The Rest of the Day Markers, colours and a full table

After all that, the little ones had the table to themselves — modelling clay in one corner, and a long row of markers laid out at the other end for drawing rainbows, suns and whatever else came to mind. None of this was on the board; it just happens. 🌈

💬 A message from teacher
The intermediate and middle school students have also been working on curriculum math problem-solving worksheets. And I also introduced cursive today, to students who were interested. It was an interesting day ❤️
🦴

Today’s learning: analogies — and the body as an analogy for a sentence, a complete thought. The middle school class explored it through analogy examples, a science experiment on eating upside down, and curriculum math problem-solving. The primary class traced a life-size body outline and learned to write the bone words, opening their sentence-building worksheet packs. Plus cursive for anyone interested — and a new class record for idioms. 💖

Ask them tonight: “What holds a sentence together?” Today they would tell you — the same thing that holds you together. Hope everyone has a wonderful afternoon. Happy Thursday! ❤️
